Game Rules

Updated for version 1.1

You'll be asked to accept these in the app before you can host or join a match.

The short version. Hunters chase runners; runners survive until the clock runs out. Everyone has hearts — lose them all and you're out. Stay inside the play area. Hunters win when every runner is caught, jailed or eliminated; runners win if one is still free at time, or if every hunter is eliminated.

Before the match

The host draws the play area, picks the mode and the match length, and assigns everyone a role: hunter, runner or spectator. A match needs at least one hunter and one runner to start (in Squad mode, runners in at least two squads).

Runners get a head start (the host sets it, up to 5 minutes; a minute by default): hunters' radar stays dark and nobody can be caught while it runs.

The host also sets the radar ping: live, or every 10 seconds up to every 10 minutes. On a live radar, every movement shows up straight away. With a ping, hunters' radar and runners' compass only update on each ping, and the screen counts down to the next one.

Hearts

Hearts are lives. Runners start with 3, hunters with 5.

  • Runners lose a heart every time they're caught.
  • Hunters lose a heart to every jailbreak.
  • Everyone loses hearts outside the play area or the shrinking zone.
  • Adrenaline adds two. Lose your last heart and you're out for the rest of the match — you stay on as a spectator.

Getting caught

  • A hunter has to get within about 15 metres of a runner and send a catch request.
  • The runner answers on their own phone within 20 seconds — in the app, or with the Yes / No buttons on the lock-screen notification. No answer counts as caught — the request already proved the hunter was close enough.
  • If the runner says no, the hunter decides: my mistake (no catch), or they insist. If they insist, the host rules. If the host is one of the two players, or doesn't rule within 45 seconds, the catch stands. If the hunter doesn't answer, the runner's word stands.
  • Every catch costs the runner one heart. Without jail mode they keep playing behind a 30-second shield to get away; with jail mode they're sent to jail. A runner who loses their last heart is out.
  • Nobody can be caught inside a Safe Zone Flare, or within 30 seconds of being freed.

Jail mode

If the host turned jail on, a caught runner with hearts left is sentenced to jail.

  • Get to the jail. Walk there yourself within the time the host set. Not turning up is a disqualification. Reconnecting starts a fresh walk; it never skips it.
  • Stay in the jail. Leaving gives you 10 seconds to get back inside, then you're disqualified.
  • The jailbreak. A free runner who stands in the jail for the time the host set breaks every prisoner out at once. It costs the rescuer a heart and takes one from every hunter. The clock only runs while somebody's inside to free, and restarts if the rescuer steps out.
  • A head start. Freed runners are shielded from catches for 30 seconds.
  • Neutral ground. The jail never costs anyone hearts — not even after the shrinking zone has passed it. Safe Zone Flares don't work at the jail, so a jailbreak is always a real risk.

The play area

The host draws the boundary, and a zone inside it closes in towards a point inside the play area, scaled to the match's length: it holds for the first fifth of the match, closes slowly and then faster, and reaches its final size — bigger for bigger areas — with a tenth of the time left. Outside either one you get a warning and then lose a heart at a time — hunters and runners alike, in every mode — until you're back inside or eliminated. Your phone's GPS accuracy is taken into account, so a bad signal at the edge won't punish you.

Power-ups

Scattered around the play area. Walk to one to pick it up, then use it from your equipment panel. You only see the ones your side can use, and you can't pick any up while caught.

Modes

  • Standard — hunters against runners, as above.
  • Infection — a caught runner switches sides and joins the hunt. Jail and hearts-on-catch don't apply. The match ends when nobody is left running.
  • Squad — teams, each with hunters and runners. You only chase other squads' runners, and your radar and compass only show the other squads. A jailbreak frees only your own squad and only costs the other squads' hunters a heart. Pick your squad when you join.

Winning

  • Hunters win when every runner has been caught, jailed or eliminated.
  • Runners win if at least one is still free when the clock hits zero, or if every hunter is eliminated.
  • Infection ends when nobody is left running.
  • Squad mode is won by the last squad with a runner still free. At time, the squad with the most runners free wins; a tie is a draw.

Playing fair, and playing safely

This game moves you through real, public places. Obey traffic laws and posted rules, don't trespass, don't play in a way that puts you or anyone else in danger, and never let the app pull your attention off your surroundings. Agree the boundaries with the people you're playing with before you start.

Don't fake your GPS, don't travel by vehicle when the match is meant to be on foot, and don't use the app's location features to follow anyone who isn't knowingly playing with you. Hosts can turn on an anti-cheat check that rejects implausible movement. Every player can be reported or blocked from their profile, and we act on reports.
